The Brentford Project is the regeneration reshaping the centre of Brentford, and its defining idea is simple: reconnect the High Street to the water it turned its back on decades ago.

The scheme, led by Ballymore, weaves new lanes and public squares between the High Street and the Thames, the Brent and the mouth of the Grand Union Canal. Alongside new homes it sets aside ground-floor units for independent retail, food and workspace — the kind of space that a bakery, a coffee roaster or a maker can actually afford to take on.

## What is open, and what is coming

Phases are opening in sequence rather than all at once. Waterfront walkways and the first public squares are the visible change today; more retail units come online through 2026. For existing High Street traders the promise is more footfall; the risk is disruption while the work continues.

## Where the card fits

New homes mean new residents who do not yet have a butcher, a barber or a local. The Brentford Card exists to shorten that gap: a single membership that points new arrivals at the independents already here.
