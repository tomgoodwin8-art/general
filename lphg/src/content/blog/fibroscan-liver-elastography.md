## What FibroScan is

FibroScan is the brand name for **transient elastography**, a painless scan that
measures how stiff the liver is. A probe rested on the skin over the right side of
the abdomen sends a gentle vibration, or shear wave, into the liver. The device
measures how fast that wave travels: the stiffer the tissue, the faster it moves.

Stiffness matters because a healthy liver is soft and elastic, while a scarred
liver becomes firmer. By measuring stiffness, FibroScan gives a non-invasive
estimate of **fibrosis** — the scarring that can build up in chronic liver
disease — without the need for a needle biopsy.

## What FibroScan shows

The scan produces two main numbers. The first is **liver stiffness**, reported in
kilopascals (kPa), which reflects the degree of fibrosis. The second, on most
machines, is the **CAP score**, measured in decibels per metre, which estimates
how much fat has built up in the liver.

Together these give a picture of both scarring and fat, the two processes that
drive most common liver conditions. Because it is quick and painless, FibroScan
can be repeated over time to track whether the liver is improving or deteriorating.

## What FibroScan cannot show

FibroScan is a measurement tool, not a full anatomical scan. It does not produce
pictures of the liver, so it cannot show gallstones, cysts, tumours or blocked
bile ducts — that is the role of a liver ultrasound. It does not identify the
cause of liver disease, whether alcohol, fatty liver, viral hepatitis or something
else.

Readings can also be unreliable in some situations, including a very high body
mass index, fluid in the abdomen, or active liver inflammation, which can
temporarily raise stiffness.

## Why a FibroScan may be requested

A FibroScan is often suggested when blood tests show abnormal liver function, when
fatty liver disease is suspected, or to monitor a known condition such as
hepatitis or previous heavy alcohol use. It is also used to decide whether more
invasive tests, such as a biopsy, are actually needed.

## How to prepare

You are usually asked not to eat for two to three hours before the scan, as food
increases blood flow through the liver and can affect the reading. Water is fine.
Wear a two-piece outfit so the right side of your abdomen and lower ribs can be
reached easily. The appointment is short and you can return to normal activity
straight away.

## Understanding your report

Your report will give a stiffness value in kPa and usually a CAP score. Lower
stiffness suggests little or no fibrosis; higher values suggest more advanced
scarring, sometimes described in stages from F0 to F4. These thresholds vary with
the underlying condition, so the numbers are always interpreted alongside your
history and blood tests rather than read in isolation.
